# High-Speed Infrared Thermometry During Polymer Deformation

**University of Massachusetts Lowell | Advanced Composite Materials & Textile Research Lab | Summer 2026**

Undergraduate Research Intern: Kiyan Heidari  
Research Advisor: Prof. Alireza V. Amirkhizi

## Overview

This project focused on developing and validating a high-speed infrared thermometry system capable of measuring transient temperature changes during mechanical deformation of polymer specimens.

Understanding temperature evolution during deformation is essential for characterizing thermomechanical coupling, plastic energy dissipation, and the Taylor–Quinney coefficient.

The research addressed limitations in optical alignment, detector sensitivity, calibration accuracy, and synchronized data acquisition.

## Research Objectives

- Design an improved optical system for high-speed infrared detection.
- Develop precision alignment methods for a detector with a 1 mm² active area.
- Calibrate infrared sensor voltage against reference temperature measurements.
- Develop a synchronized data acquisition framework for experimental testing.
- Validate temperature measurement during quasi-static tensile deformation.
- Establish a foundation for future high-strain-rate testing using a Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar (SHPB).

## Key Contributions

### Optical System Development
Evaluated limitations of the original multi-component optical configuration and developed a simplified approach to improve alignment repeatability and signal collection.

### Uncertainty Quantification
Applied dimensional tolerance analysis and Monte Carlo simulation to evaluate the effects of manufacturing and assembly uncertainty on optical alignment.

### Sensor Calibration
Established an experimental calibration relationship between infrared sensor voltage and reference temperature measurements.

The reported quadratic calibration achieved an R² of 0.9912.
